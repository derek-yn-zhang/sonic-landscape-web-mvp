About This Analysis

This visualization explores your music collection through five perceptual dimensions that describe the sonic character of each track. Each axis captures a distinct quality of how music sounds and feels, derived from audio signal analysis and research-backed acoustic features.

Using Principal Component Analysis (PCA), we discovered that these five axes actually capture four relatively independent sonic qualities that explain most of the variation in your collection. Machine learning clustering then groups tracks by their acoustic similarities.

The Five Axes

Warmth
Tonal color from bright/cold to warm/rich. Derived from spectral centroid and timbral warmth models.
Spatiality
Sense of space from intimate to expansive. Measured through stereo width and channel correlation.
Distance
Perceived proximity from close/present to far/diffuse. Based on spectral rolloff and dynamic range as proxies for direct-to-reverberant ratio.
Density
Textural fullness from sparse to thick/layered. Combines spectral bandwidth, flatness, and timbral depth.
Groundedness
Rhythmic foundation from floating/ambient to anchored/driving. Primarily pulse clarity and beat salience, not just bass presence.

The Sonic Dimensions

PCA reveals that these 5 axes actually capture 4 relatively independent qualities. Here's what they mean musically:

PC1: Warmth-Density Spectrum 52% of variation
The biggest differentiator in this collection. Are you in the mood for something lush and open, or something textured and intense?
warm, distant, sparse ↔ cool, close, dense
PC2: Spatial Character 21% of variation
Whether a track feels "out there" in a vast space or "right here" in front of you.
spacious, cool, floating ↔ intimate, warm, grounded
PC3: Rhythmic Space 16% of variation
Groundedness and spatiality load together here - can a track be groovy AND expansive?
rhythmic + wide stereo ↔ ambient + focused
PC4: Textural Complexity 8% of variation
Almost purely density - how layered and complex the sound is, independent of the other qualities.
sparse, minimal ↔ dense, layered

Principal Component Analysis reduces 5 dimensions to 2 for visualization. PC1 explains 52.3% of variance, PC2 explains 20.7%. Arrows show how each axis contributes to the principal components.

Radar charts show each cluster's profile across all five terrain dimensions. This reveals what makes each cluster distinct - where it scores high and low on each axis.

Cluster Characteristics

Component loadings show how each axis contributes to the principal components. Positive values indicate positive correlation, negative values indicate inverse correlation.
